Integrated County Assembly Management System (ICAMS)

A proposed web-based platform for organizing county assembly operations. The public repository and résumé describe its scope; planned capabilities below are not claims that every module is complete or deployed.

Purpose and Context

ICAMS is intended to bring common administrative workflows into one coordinated system. The project addresses areas such as users and roles, documents, attendance, meetings, committees, assets, support requests, internships, and public participation. Its goal is to make information easier to record, find, and follow through an organization’s workflow.

Planned and Documented Modules

  • Authentication and user management: sign-in and role-based access for different user groups.
  • Document tracking: record document details, upload files, search and filter records, and track review, approval, movement, and archive history.
  • Attendance and leave: record attendance, check-in/check-out activity, summaries, and leave requests.
  • Meetings, committees, and visitors: organize relevant records and operational information.
  • Assets, helpdesk, and internships: support asset records, complaints or support tickets, and internship administration.
  • Public participation: provide a planned way to share information and receive public feedback.

Technology Recorded for the Project

The résumé lists React, Node.js, Express, MongoDB or PostgreSQL, JWT authentication, and Chart.js. The public repository describes a React client, an Express backend, MongoDB integration, and authentication-related components. Confirm the current code before treating any planned module or alternative database as a completed production feature.

My Role and Learning Goals

I am developing my software engineering experience through this project. It gives me an opportunity to think about how roles, workflows, document records, attendance, and reporting fit together in a complex organization. My areas of focus include organizing backend routes and data models, connecting user interfaces to APIs, considering access control, and communicating project scope clearly.

Specific measured outcomes, deployment status, and a complete list of individually implemented features were not supplied in my résumé. I therefore describe ICAMS as proposed project work and do not claim performance results.
